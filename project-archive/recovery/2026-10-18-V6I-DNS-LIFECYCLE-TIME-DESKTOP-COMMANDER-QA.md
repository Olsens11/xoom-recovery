## DNS init service lifecycle — physical RAM-only controlled QA

At initial baseline, the Xoom enumerated over USB ADB, `init.svc.xoomdns=running`, `xoom.n2.wifi.status=WIFI_READY`, exact v0.5.2 production proxy SHA-256 `542b9238b872a6e2c4e4b5ca6873dc1f872e626b65bfa1b2754f0614ddbfa46a`, and a live UNIX socket under `/dev/socket/dnsproxyd` that appeared in `/proc/net/unix`.

A guarded temporary test script in recovery RAM requested ordinary `ctl.stop xoomdns`. Init reported `stopped`, the proxy process was absent, and the socket address was no longer bound/listed in `/proc/net/unix`, **but the root-owned UNIX socket pathname persisted on the filesystem**. The device logged the exact evidence sentinel `OBSERVED_STALE_SOCKET_AFTER_INIT_STOP`. This reproduces the stale-name defect discovered during v6H RAM QA. After verifying no socket owner/PID existed, correct socket type and root ownership, and the unchanged trusted ELF, the script removed **only the stale /dev/socket/dnsproxyd pathname**, then requested `ctl.start xoomdns`. Init returned **running**, the bound socket reappeared, the exact ELF hash still matched, and Wi-Fi remained `WIFI_READY`. Test sentinel: `V6I_DNS_INIT_RESTART_RAM_QA_PASS`.

A subsequent **on-Xoom BusyBox Bionic-built** hostname ping of `example.org` resolved and received a reply, with Wi-Fi still `WIFI_READY` and DNS still `running`. This verifies service recovery after **manual guarded stale pathname removal**, NOT successful hands-off stop/restart. No persistent state, package or recovery partition was changed. **Required future engineering:** repair stale socket cleanup (prefer at proper owner/service lifecycle boundary) and test unattended restart without intervention; do not regress clean v6I startup.
